Page 02 — Diagnosis

Sugarcane doesn't need water on a schedule. It needs water on time.

Most irrigation in sugarcane fields still runs on a fixed calendar — every 10 or 15 days, regardless of what the soil, the weather, or the crop itself is actually asking for. That mismatch is where the water, the yield, and the farmer's margin get lost.

The core issue

A sugarcane crop's water demand changes by the week, but a calendar doesn't. The result is a crop that's flooded when it needs little, and starved when it needs the most.

Root causes

Why the calendar approach persists anyway.

01

No visibility into the soil

Without sensors, moisture is judged by eye or by touch — both unreliable, and both blind to conditions a few inches below the surface where cane roots actually draw water.

02

Weather is treated as background noise

A schedule set in June doesn't adjust for an unseasonal dry spell in August or an early monsoon burst — both of which change how much irrigation the field actually needs that week.

03

Every plot is treated the same

Soil type, drainage, and shade vary even within a single field, but a fixed schedule applies one irrigation volume across the entire plot, regardless of these differences.

04

Advisory tools stop at general guidelines

Most extension advice offers season-long norms, not a live, field-specific answer to the question a farmer actually has each morning: irrigate today, or not?
