Page 03 — How it works

Sensors that listen to the field, and a model that decides.

Every recommendation starts as a raw electrical signal in the soil. Here's the full path it takes — from sensor to gateway to AI model to the advisory a farmer actually sees.

The sensor layer

What's actually installed in the field.

A compact node placed per representative plot, low-power and solar-assisted for continuous field deployment.

SEN · 01

Soil moisture probe

Capacitive sensor reading volumetric water content at root depth, logged every 30 minutes.

SEN · 02

Soil temperature probe

Paired with the moisture probe to track how heat affects evapotranspiration through the day.

SEN · 03

Ambient humidity & temp

Above-canopy readings used alongside forecast data to estimate near-term water loss.

SEN · 04

Rain gauge

Tips-bucket rainfall sensor so recent natural rainfall is subtracted from irrigation recommendations.

The prediction model

What the AI is actually doing with the data.

The model's job is narrow and specific: given today's soil and weather readings plus the crop's current growth stage, predict whether the field's moisture will fall below the safe threshold for that stage within the next 24–48 hours.

It's trained on historical sensor logs paired with recorded irrigation outcomes, so its recommendations reflect how this specific soil type and plot actually respond to water — not a generic crop-water table.

Where confidence is low, such as right after unseasonal rainfall, the model flags the recommendation as provisional rather than presenting it as certain.

Model type Supervised regression / classification ensemble
Inputs Soil moisture, soil temp, humidity, rainfall, growth stage, 48-hr forecast
Output Irrigate now / hold / reduce, with a confidence level
Update frequency Recalculated every reading cycle (30 min)
Training data Historical plot-level sensor logs + irrigation records
